# Aeronautics — Expert Knowledge Base

## What Is Aeronautics?

Aeronautics is the science and practice of flight within Earth's atmosphere. It encompasses the design, construction, and operation of aircraft, as well as the study of the physical principles that make flight possible. Aeronautics draws on fluid dynamics, thermodynamics, materials science, structural engineering, avionics, and human factors.

## The Four Forces of Flight

Every aircraft in steady, level flight is in equilibrium of four forces:

```
                    LIFT ↑
                      |
DRAG ←————— AIRCRAFT ————→ THRUST
                      |
                  WEIGHT ↓
```

### Lift
- The upward aerodynamic force generated by wings
- Bernoulli's principle: faster air over curved upper surface = lower pressure above wing = net upward force
- Newton's third law: wing deflects air downward; equal and opposite reaction lifts wing
- **Lift formula**: L = ½ρv²SC_L
  - ρ = air density, v = velocity, S = wing area, C_L = coefficient of lift

### Drag
- The resistance force opposing motion through the air
- **Parasite drag**: Friction and pressure drag from aircraft body (increases with speed)
- **Induced drag**: Drag created as byproduct of lift generation (decreases with speed)
- **Total drag**: Sum has a minimum at a specific airspeed (best glide speed)

### Thrust
- The force propelling the aircraft forward
- Sources: propeller, jet engine, rocket

### Weight
- Gravitational force pulling aircraft toward Earth
- Always acts toward Earth's center
- Must be overcome by lift for flight

## Airfoil and Wing Design

### Airfoil Cross-Section
- **Chord line**: Straight line from leading to trailing edge
- **Camber**: Curvature of the airfoil (upper surface more curved for lift)
- **Thickness**: Affects drag and structural strength
- **Angle of attack (AOA)**: Angle between chord line and relative wind

### Stall
- When angle of attack becomes too high, airflow separates from wing → lift collapses
- **Critical angle of attack**: Typically 16–20° for most airfoils
- Stall speed depends on weight, bank angle, and configuration (flaps, etc.)
- Accelerated stall: Can occur at any speed if AOA is too high

### Wing Geometry
- **Aspect ratio**: Wingspan² / wing area; high AR (glider) = low induced drag; low AR (fighter) = high maneuverability
- **Sweep**: Swept wings delay onset of compressibility effects at high speed
- **Dihedral**: Upward angle from root to tip; provides roll stability
- **Winglets**: Upward-bent wingtips reduce induced drag (10–15% fuel savings on airliners)

## Aircraft Performance

### Speed
- **Indicated Airspeed (IAS)**: What the airspeed indicator reads (dynamic pressure)
- **True Airspeed (TAS)**: Actual speed through the air mass (increases with altitude for same IAS)
- **Groundspeed**: Speed over the ground (TAS ± wind)
- **Mach number**: Speed as fraction of speed of sound (~661 kt at sea level; varies with temperature)

### Performance Envelope
- **VNE**: Never Exceed speed (redline)
- **VA**: Maneuvering speed (maximum for full control deflection)
- **VFE**: Maximum flap extended speed
- **VS**: Stall speed (varies with configuration)

### Climb Performance
- **Best rate of climb (Vy)**: Maximum altitude per unit time
- **Best angle of climb (Vx)**: Maximum altitude per unit distance (obstacle clearance)
- **Service ceiling**: Altitude where max climb rate = 100 ft/min
- **Absolute ceiling**: Maximum altitude where aircraft can maintain level flight (theoretical)

## Propulsion Systems

### Piston Engines (Reciprocating)
- Traditional aircraft engine (Cessna, Piper, Beechcraft)
- 4-stroke cycle: intake → compression → power → exhaust
- Opposed cylinder configurations most common in aviation (flat-4, flat-6)
- Horizontally opposed: Lycoming, Continental
- Fuel: 100LL (low-lead avgas); moving toward unleaded alternatives

### Turboprop Engines
- Jet engine drives a propeller through a reduction gearbox
- Efficient at low-to-medium altitude and speed (250–400 kt)
- Examples: King Air (PT6 engine), ATR 42/72, C-130 Hercules

### Turbojet Engines
- Air compressed → mixed with fuel → ignited → hot exhaust provides thrust
- High speed but fuel-inefficient at subsonic speeds
- Used in early jets; now largely replaced by turbofans

### Turbofan Engines (Modern Airliners)
- Turbojet + large fan at front; fan moves most of air around the core ("bypass air")
- **Bypass ratio**: Ratio of bypass air to core air (modern: 10:1 or higher)
- High bypass = more efficient, quieter; low bypass = higher thrust, better for supersonic
- Examples: CFM56 (737, A320), GE9X (787), Trent XWB (A350)

### Electric Propulsion
- Battery-electric: Pipistrel Velis Electro (first certified electric aircraft); limited range
- Hybrid-electric: Combustion + electric; range extension
- Hydrogen fuel cell: Zero-emission; water vapor exhaust; infrastructure challenges
- eVTOL: Electric vertical takeoff and landing; Joby Aviation, Archer, Lilium (Urban Air Mobility)

## Avionics and Navigation

### Navigation Systems
- **VOR (VHF Omnidirectional Range)**: Ground-based radio navigation; gives magnetic radial from station
- **ILS (Instrument Landing System)**: Guides aircraft to precision landing in low visibility
- **GPS/GNSS**: Global positioning; primary navigation in modern aviation
- **INS/IRS (Inertial Navigation/Reference System)**: Accelerometer-based; independent of external signals
- **ADS-B**: GPS position broadcast; replaces radar for surveillance; mandatory in US since 2020

### Flight Instruments (The "Six Pack")
1. **Airspeed indicator** — IAS in knots
2. **Attitude indicator** — pitch and bank relative to horizon (gyroscope)
3. **Altimeter** — barometric altitude
4. **Turn coordinator** — rate of turn and coordination
5. **Heading indicator** — magnetic heading (gyroscope)
6. **Vertical speed indicator** — rate of climb/descent

### Glass Cockpit (Modern)
- Primary Flight Display (PFD): Replaces 6 pack with single multifunction display
- Multi-Function Display (MFD): Navigation, weather, traffic, engine data
- Synthetic vision: 3D terrain rendering for situational awareness

## Meteorology for Pilots

### Aviation Weather Hazards
- **Thunderstorms**: Convective turbulence, hail, lightning, wind shear, icing; avoid by 20+ miles
- **Icing**: Structural icing on wings/tail changes airfoil shape → increases stall speed, reduces lift
- **Wind shear**: Sudden change in wind speed or direction; most dangerous during approach
- **Microburst**: Intense downdraft + outflow; can cause immediate loss of altitude
- **Fog**: Ground or advection fog; reduces visibility below VFR minimums

### Weather Products for Pilots
- **METAR**: Hourly surface observations at airports
- **TAF**: Terminal Aerodrome Forecast; 24–30 hour forecast
- **PIREPs**: Pilot reports (turbulence, icing, cloud tops)
- **SIGMETs**: Significant meteorological information (severe icing, turbulence, volcanic ash)
- **Winds Aloft**: Forecast winds and temperatures at various altitudes

## Aircraft Categories and Ratings (FAA, US)

### Pilot Certificates
| Certificate | Privileges | Requirements |
|-------------|-----------|-------------|
| Student | Solo flight with limitations | Medical + ground training |
| Private (PPL) | VFR flight; carry passengers | 40+ hours; written + practical tests |
| Instrument (IFR) | Fly in clouds; instrument approaches | 50 hrs XC PIC; 40 hrs instrument |
| Commercial (CPL) | Fly for compensation | 250 hours |
| ATP | Fly airline transport | 1,500 hours (1,000 with military) |

### Aircraft Categories
- **Airplane** (fixed wing): single engine land/sea, multi-engine land/sea
- **Rotorcraft**: helicopter, gyroplane
- **Lighter than air**: balloon, airship
- **Powered lift**: tiltrotor (V-22 Osprey)
- **Glider**: no engine; soaring using thermals and ridge lift

## Aerodynamics at High Speed

### Compressibility Effects (Transonic/Supersonic)
- At Mach 0.75+, shock waves begin forming on the wing
- Shock waves cause sudden increase in drag ("wave drag")
- **Sound barrier**: Not a physical barrier but rapid drag increase at Mach 1
- **Critical Mach number**: Speed at which local airflow first reaches Mach 1 somewhere on wing

### Sonic Boom
- Continuous shockwave cone follows supersonic aircraft
- Boom heard at ground as aircraft passes overhead (not when it breaks sound barrier)
- Sonic boom is a major constraint on commercial supersonic flight over land

### Supersonic Aircraft Design
- Thin, swept/delta wings minimize wave drag
- Area ruling ("Coke bottle" fuselage): smooth cross-sectional area distribution reduces transonic drag
- Variable geometry wings: F-111, B-1 (sweep changes for different flight regimes)

## Unmanned Aircraft Systems (UAS / Drones) in Aeronautics Context

### FAA Regulations (US)
- Part 107: Commercial UAS rule; sUAS under 55 lbs; remote pilot certificate required
- Line of sight requirement; altitude limited to 400 ft AGL
- Controlled airspace requires LAANC authorization
- Remote ID: Required since 2023; aircraft must broadcast identity and position

### Categories of UAS Operations
- **Recreational**: Hobbyist flying; FAA registration; safety guidelines
- **Commercial**: Part 107; weather observation, inspection, agriculture, photography
- **Public safety**: Fire, law enforcement, search and rescue
- **Defense**: Intelligence, surveillance, reconnaissance (ISR); strike (Predator, Reaper, MQ-25)
