# Military — Expert Knowledge Base

## The Role of Military Force

Military force is the ultimate arbiter of sovereignty, security, and geopolitical competition. Diplomacy exists in the shadow of military capability — a nation without military power negotiates from weakness. Understanding military doctrine, history, and technology is essential to understanding how the world actually works.

> "Si vis pacem, para bellum." (If you want peace, prepare for war.) — Roman adage

---

## Levels of War

### Strategic Level
- Highest level; concerns national objectives and the use of all instruments of national power
- Translates political objectives into military strategy
- Examples: US strategy to contain Soviet expansion; Hitler's two-front war mistake

### Operational Level
- Bridges strategy and tactics
- Campaign planning: sequencing of battles and operations to achieve strategic objectives
- Theater-level commanders (Eisenhower in Europe, MacArthur in the Pacific)

### Tactical Level
- Employment of forces in actual combat
- Small unit actions: squad, platoon, company, battalion level
- Fire, maneuver, cover, concealment

## Principles of War (US Army FM 3-0)

1. **Objective**: Direct military operations toward defined, decisive goals
2. **Offensive**: Seize, retain, and exploit the initiative
3. **Mass**: Concentrate combat power at decisive times and places
4. **Economy of Force**: Allocate minimum essential combat power to secondary efforts
5. **Maneuver**: Place the enemy in a position of disadvantage through flexible application of force
6. **Unity of Command**: Single commander for each objective
7. **Security**: Never permit the enemy to acquire unexpected advantage
8. **Surprise**: Strike the enemy at a time, place, or manner for which they are unprepared
9. **Simplicity**: Prepare clear, uncomplicated plans and clear, concise orders

## Military Strategy Frameworks

### Attrition Warfare
- Wear down the enemy through superior resources and casualties
- WWI Western Front; Grant's strategy in Civil War
- Works if you have more resources than the enemy; extremely costly

### Maneuver Warfare
- Win by disrupting enemy's decision-making cycle, not by destroying forces
- OODA loop advantage: faster observe-orient-decide-act cycle
- Blitzkrieg, Gulf War (Operation Desert Storm)
- Aims to paralyze, not just kill

### Guerrilla Warfare / Insurgency
- Asymmetric: weaker force uses unconventional tactics against conventional military
- "The fish swims in the sea of the people" — Mao Zedong
- Vietnam War, Afghan Mujahideen, Iraq insurgency
- Goals: deny victory, raise costs, outlast the opponent

### Airpower Theory (Douhet, Mitchell)
- Strategic bombing can win wars independently
- Attack industrial base, population morale, supply lines
- WWII: bombing did not break German morale; contributed to war effort but didn't win alone
- Modern evolution: precision strike capability changes the calculus

### Naval Strategy (Mahan)
- Control of sea lanes = control of commerce = global power
- Maritime supremacy enables force projection anywhere on earth
- US carrier battle groups: 11 active carriers; no other nation has more than 2

## Nuclear Strategy

### The Nuclear Trinity
- **Mutual Assured Destruction (MAD)**: Both sides maintain second-strike capability → neither dares first strike
- **Second-strike capability**: Ability to absorb a nuclear first strike and retaliate devastatingly
- **The triad**: Land-based ICBMs + submarine-launched ballistic missiles (SLBMs) + strategic bombers

### Nuclear Weapons States
- Declared: US, Russia, UK, France, China (NPT signatories)
- Undeclared: India, Pakistan, Israel (never confirmed), North Korea
- ~12,500 nuclear warheads worldwide; ~90% held by US and Russia

### Escalation Ladder
- Conventional warfare → tactical nuclear weapons → theater nuclear → strategic nuclear
- De-escalation: nuclear-armed states generally avoid direct conventional conflict (proxy wars instead)

### Nuclear Deterrence Theory
- **Extended deterrence**: US nuclear umbrella over allies (Japan, South Korea, NATO)
- **Minimum deterrence**: Small arsenals sufficient for credible second strike (China's doctrine)
- **Launch-on-warning**: Responding to detected attack before missiles land (dangerous with false alarms)

## Military Branches (US)

### Army
- Ground combat force; largest branch
- Mission: deploy, fight, and win the nation's wars
- Combined arms: infantry + armor + artillery + aviation + engineers + logistics

### Navy
- Controls sea lanes; power projection via carrier battle groups
- Submarines: Ohio-class SSBNs (nuclear deterrent); Virginia-class SSNs (attack)
- Surface warfare: destroyers, cruisers, carriers

### Air Force
- Air superiority, strategic bombing, close air support, airlift, ISR
- F-22 (air superiority), F-35 (multirole), B-2 (stealth bomber), B-52 (long-range conventional/nuclear)
- Space Force (newest branch, 2019): military space operations

### Marine Corps
- Expeditionary force-in-readiness; amphibious assault
- Operates as part of Department of the Navy
- Force design 2030: restructuring away from tanks toward distributed littoral combat

### Special Operations Forces (SOF)
- USSOCOM: umbrella command for all SOF
- Army: Rangers, Green Berets (Special Forces), Delta Force (1st SFOD-D)
- Navy: SEALs, DEVGRU (SEAL Team 6)
- Air Force: Combat Controllers, PJs, AFSOC
- Marine Raiders, MARSOC

## Intelligence in Warfare

### Types of Intelligence (INTS)
- **HUMINT** (Human Intelligence): Spies, informants, interrogation
- **SIGINT** (Signals Intelligence): Communications intercepts, electronic emissions
- **IMINT** (Imagery Intelligence): Satellite/aerial photography
- **OSINT** (Open Source Intelligence): Public information analysis
- **GEOINT** (Geospatial Intelligence): Location and terrain data
- **MASINT** (Measurement and Signature): Sensor data (seismic, chemical, nuclear)

### Intelligence Cycle
1. Planning and direction
2. Collection
3. Processing and exploitation
4. Analysis and production
5. Dissemination

## Modern Warfare Domains

### Cyber Warfare
- Attacks on adversary's information infrastructure, power grids, financial systems
- Stuxnet (US/Israel vs. Iran): first known cyberweapon to cause physical destruction
- SolarWinds (Russia vs. US): supply chain attack penetrated government networks
- Offensive cyber capability equals military power in the 21st century

### Space Warfare
- Anti-satellite weapons (ASAT): US, Russia, China, India have tested
- GPS jamming and spoofing
- Satellite killers (direct ascent, co-orbital, directed energy)
- China's PLA Strategic Support Force: space, cyber, and electronic warfare

### Electronic Warfare (EW)
- Jamming, spoofing, electromagnetic pulse (EMP)
- EMP (nuclear or non-nuclear): destroys unshielded electronics over wide area
- Electronic attack, electronic protection, electronic support

### Drone Warfare
- Unmanned aerial systems (UAS): from Predator/Reaper to FPV suicide drones
- Ukraine war: democratized drone warfare — cheap commercial drones reshaped the battlefield
- Drone swarms: future systems using AI coordination

### Information Warfare / Psychological Operations (PSYOP)
- Influence operations targeting adversary decision-makers and populations
- Active measures (Soviet/Russian term): disinformation, propaganda, subversion
- Social media weaponization: Internet Research Agency (Russia), etc.

## Major Wars and Their Lessons

### World War I (1914–1918)
- Industrial warfare: machine guns + artillery + static trenches = mass slaughter
- Lesson: attritional warfare without decisive breakthrough creates catastrophic stalemate
- 20 million dead; collapse of four empires (Ottoman, Austrian, Russian, German)

### World War II (1939–1945)
- Combined arms warfare, strategic bombing, amphibious operations
- Lesson: ideological wars are total wars; modern logistics wins modern wars
- 70–85 million dead; US emerges as the sole nuclear power

### Korean War (1950–1953)
- Lesson: Limited war under nuclear shadow; importance of alliance credibility
- MacArthur relieved for insubordination — civilian control of military reinforced

### Vietnam War (1955–1975)
- US: technologically superior but strategically defeated
- Lesson: Counterinsurgency requires political legitimacy, not just firepower; body counts are not victory metrics

### Gulf War (1990–1991)
- First demonstration of modern precision warfare (smart bombs, stealth, GPS)
- 100-hour ground war after 6 weeks of air campaign
- Lesson: overwhelming conventional superiority can achieve rapid victory against conventional forces

### Global War on Terror (2001–present)
- Afghanistan: longest US war; government collapsed days after US withdrawal (2021)
- Iraq: successful invasion but failed occupation; ISIS emerged from the chaos
- Lesson: Military victory in conventional war does not guarantee political success

## The Military-Industrial Complex

- Eisenhower warned of it in 1961 farewell address
- Defense contractors (Lockheed Martin, Raytheon, Boeing, Northrop Grumman, General Dynamics) exert massive influence on defense policy
- "Iron triangle": defense contractors + Pentagon + Congressional defense committees = self-reinforcing spending system
- Revolving door: military officers retire to contractor jobs; contractors lobby for contracts; congressmen seek contractor jobs in their districts
