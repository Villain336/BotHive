# Wealth — Expert Knowledge Base

## Core Definition

Wealth is not income. Wealth is the accumulation of assets that generate value independent of active labor. The wealthy own things that work for them; the non-wealthy trade time for money. True wealth is measured in time — how long you can sustain your lifestyle without working.

---

## The Three Sources of Wealth

### 1. Ownership
- Equity in businesses (private or public)
- Real estate and land
- Intellectual property (patents, royalties, trademarks)
- Natural resources

### 2. Leverage
- Other people's time (employees, contractors)
- Other people's money (debt, investors, float)
- Technology (software, automation, AI)
- Media (content that earns while you sleep)

### 3. Compounding
- Returns reinvested grow exponentially, not linearly
- The Rule of 72: divide 72 by your annual return rate to find years to double money
- Time in the market consistently beats timing the market
- Warren Buffett earned 99% of his wealth after age 50 — compounding is time-dependent

---

## Wealth Principles

### The Pareto Principle (80/20)
- 80% of your wealth will come from 20% of your decisions
- Focus relentlessly on high-leverage activities
- Ruthlessly eliminate low-value tasks

### Wealth vs. Income
| Income | Wealth |
|--------|--------|
| Active (requires labor) | Passive (requires assets) |
| Taxed at highest rates | Taxed at preferential rates (capital gains) |
| Stops when you stop | Continues indefinitely |
| Vulnerable to layoff | Protected by diversification |

### The Seven Pillars of Wealth Building
1. **Earn more** — increase income through skill, value creation, negotiation
2. **Spend less** — live below your means; avoid lifestyle inflation
3. **Invest the difference** — put surplus capital to work
4. **Protect assets** — legal structures, insurance, diversification
5. **Optimize taxes** — legally minimize tax burden at every step
6. **Build systems** — create income streams not dependent on your presence
7. **Network deliberately** — your net worth tracks your network

## Wealth Archetypes

### The Entrepreneur
Builds equity by creating systems and companies. High risk, high reward. The greatest wealth generator in history — most billionaires are founders.

### The Investor
Deploys capital into assets. Buys ownership stakes at a discount to intrinsic value. Patience and temperament are the primary skill set.

### The Professional
Monetizes expertise. Lawyers, surgeons, consultants. Cap on income unless they transition to owning their practice or licensing their knowledge.

### The Inheritor
Steward of generational wealth. Most fortunes are lost by the third generation (shirtsleeves to shirtsleeves in three generations). Financial education is the differentiator.

## Historical Wealth Mechanisms

- **Land enclosure** (England, 1500s–1800s): Controlling scarce land = controlling the population
- **Railroads** (USA, 1800s): Infrastructure monopoly — whoever owns the track owns commerce
- **Standard Oil** (Rockefeller): Vertical integration — own the wells, refineries, pipelines, and retail
- **Carnegie Steel**: Cost leadership through technology + vertical integration
- **Ford**: Mass production made luxury accessible and captured a mass market
- **Bezos/Amazon**: Platform business — Amazon didn't just sell books; it became the infrastructure of e-commerce

## Mindset of the Wealthy

- **Abundance mindset**: Opportunity is everywhere; competition is irrelevant if you create unique value
- **Long-term orientation**: Sacrifice short-term pleasure for long-term gain
- **Asset thinking**: Always ask "does this make me money or cost me money?"
- **Tax efficiency**: The wealthy don't earn income — they borrow against appreciating assets (the "Buy, Borrow, Die" strategy)
- **Optionality**: Keep options open; never box yourself into one path

---

## The "Buy, Borrow, Die" Strategy

The ultra-wealthy avoid income tax legally:
1. **Buy** appreciating assets (stocks, real estate, businesses)
2. **Borrow** against those assets (loans are not taxable income)
3. **Die** and pass assets to heirs at stepped-up cost basis (eliminating capital gains tax)

This allows perpetual access to capital without triggering a taxable event.

## Wealth Killers

- Lifestyle inflation (increasing spending as income rises)
- High-interest consumer debt
- Tax inefficiency (paying more than legally required)
- Lack of diversification (concentration risk)
- Emotional investing (buying high, selling low)
- No estate plan (wealth destroyed at death through probate and taxes)
- Trusting the wrong people (fraud, bad advisors)

---

## Generational Wealth

- **Entity structures**: Trusts, family limited partnerships, LLCs protect and transfer wealth
- **The Dynasty Trust**: Some states allow perpetual trusts — wealth can compound for hundreds of years
- **Education**: Teaching financial literacy to children is more valuable than simply giving them money
- **Shared purpose**: Family offices and family constitutions align wealthy families around shared values
