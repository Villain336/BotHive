# Business — Expert Knowledge Base

## The Nature of Business

A business is a system that delivers value to customers in exchange for revenue that exceeds costs. The goal is not just to generate income — it is to build a **self-sustaining, scalable system** that creates value without requiring the founder's constant presence.

## Business Structures

| Structure | Liability | Taxation | Best For |
|-----------|-----------|----------|----------|
| Sole Proprietorship | Unlimited personal | Pass-through | Side hustle, freelancers |
| Partnership | Shared personal | Pass-through | Professional firms |
| LLC | Limited | Pass-through or corporate | Most small businesses |
| S-Corp | Limited | Pass-through | Avoiding self-employment tax |
| C-Corp | Limited | Double taxation | VC-backed startups, public cos |
| Nonprofit (501c3) | Limited | Tax-exempt | Mission-driven organizations |

### Why LLC + S-Corp Election?
The optimal structure for most small businesses:
- LLC protects personal assets
- S-Corp election allows owner to take reasonable salary + distributions
- Distributions avoid 15.3% self-employment tax
- Example: $200K profit → $60K salary (taxed fully) + $140K distribution (no SE tax)

## The Business Model Framework

### Revenue Models
- **Product sales**: One-time transaction
- **Subscription/SaaS**: Recurring revenue — most valuable per-dollar of revenue
- **Marketplace**: Take a percentage of transactions (Airbnb, Uber, eBay)
- **Freemium**: Free tier → convert to paid
- **Licensing**: Charge for use of IP
- **Advertising**: Sell attention to advertisers
- **Franchise**: License business model + brand to operators
- **Consulting/Service**: Charge for expertise and time

### The Subscription Model Advantage
- Predictable revenue (MRR/ARR)
- High customer lifetime value (LTV)
- Easier to forecast and raise capital
- Churn is the primary enemy — focus on retention

## Starting a Business

### The Lean Startup Method (Eric Ries)
1. Build a Minimum Viable Product (MVP)
2. Measure customer response
3. Learn and iterate
4. Avoid building what nobody wants

### Validation Before Building
- Pre-sell before you build
- Use landing pages to test demand (collect emails/deposits)
- Interview potential customers — listen for problems, not solutions
- The customer's willingness to pay is the only real validation

### The Business Plan (Simplified)
1. **Problem**: What pain does this solve?
2. **Solution**: How does your product/service solve it?
3. **Market**: How big is the opportunity?
4. **Competition**: Who else is solving this and how are you different?
5. **Business model**: How do you make money?
6. **Go-to-market**: How do you acquire customers?
7. **Team**: Why are you the right people to execute this?
8. **Financials**: Revenue projections, unit economics, funding needs

## Unit Economics

The foundation of a sustainable business:

### Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC)
```
CAC = Total Sales & Marketing Spend / Number of New Customers
```

### Customer Lifetime Value (LTV)
```
LTV = Average Purchase Value × Purchase Frequency × Customer Lifespan
```

### The Golden Ratio
```
LTV / CAC > 3 = Healthy business
LTV / CAC < 1 = Burning money
```

### Gross Margin
```
Gross Margin = (Revenue - COGS) / Revenue
```
- Software: 70–90% (extremely scalable)
- Services: 40–60%
- Physical products: 20–50%
- Restaurants: 60–70% gross margin but high overhead

## Marketing Fundamentals

### The Marketing Funnel
1. **Awareness**: Target audience knows you exist
2. **Interest**: Educating prospects about your solution
3. **Consideration**: Comparing you to alternatives
4. **Intent**: Ready to buy
5. **Purchase**: Transaction occurs
6. **Loyalty**: Repeat purchase + referrals

### Acquisition Channels
- **SEO**: Long-term, compounding, high trust
- **Paid ads**: Fast, scalable, immediate feedback (Google, Meta, TikTok)
- **Content marketing**: Authority building, SEO foundation
- **Social media**: Brand building, community
- **Email marketing**: Highest ROI channel (average $36 return per $1 spent)
- **Referral/word of mouth**: Lowest CAC, highest trust
- **Partnership/affiliate**: Pay per acquisition
- **Cold outreach**: B2B staple — cold email, LinkedIn DM

### Positioning (April Dunford)
- **Who** is the customer?
- **What** is the competitive alternative?
- **What** unique attributes do you have?
- **What value** do those attributes deliver?
- **Who** specifically values that most?

## Sales

### The Sales Process (B2B)
1. Prospecting — find qualified leads
2. Discovery — understand their pain, budget, authority, timeline (BANT)
3. Demonstration — show your solution solves their specific problem
4. Objection handling — address concerns without discounting
5. Closing — ask for the business
6. Follow-up — most deals close after 5+ touchpoints

### Pricing Psychology
- **Anchoring**: Show a high price first; the real price feels reasonable
- **Charm pricing**: $97 vs. $100 — significant psychological difference
- **Tiered pricing**: Good/Better/Best — middle option drives most revenue
- **Value-based pricing**: Charge based on value delivered, not cost-plus
- **Loss aversion**: Frame as avoiding loss, not gaining benefit

## Operations and Systems

### The E-Myth (Michael Gerber)
Most business owners work IN their business, not ON it. They own a job, not a business. The goal is to systematize every process so anyone can execute it.

### Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs)
- Document every repeatable process
- Video + written instructions
- Enables delegation, hiring, and scaling
- Example: McDonald's — a teenager can run a franchise because of SOPs

### Key Operations Metrics
- **Burn rate**: Monthly cash outflow
- **Runway**: Cash / Burn rate = months until broke
- **EBITDA**: Earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, amortization
- **Churn rate**: % of customers lost per period
- **NPS (Net Promoter Score)**: "How likely are you to recommend us?" (-100 to +100)

## Growth Strategies

### Organic Growth
- Build audience before building product
- Content-led growth (SEO, YouTube, podcast)
- Community-led growth (Discord, forums, events)
- Product-led growth (viral loops built into product — Dropbox referral)

### Inorganic Growth
- **M&A**: Acquire competitors or complementary businesses
- **Roll-up**: Buy fragmented industry players, consolidate, improve margins
- **Licensing**: License your brand/IP for royalty income without operating costs

### Network Effects (The Moat)
A business becomes more valuable as more people use it:
- **Direct network effects**: Telephone — more users = more valuable for everyone
- **Indirect network effects**: Marketplace — more sellers attract buyers; more buyers attract sellers
- **Data network effects**: More users → more data → better product → more users

## Raising Capital

### Bootstrapping
- Self-funded; no dilution; full control
- Revenue is the best funding
- Constraints force creativity

### Angel Investors
- High-net-worth individuals investing personal money
- Typical check: $25K–$500K
- Pre-seed and seed stage
- Access through AngelList, local networks, warm introductions

### Venture Capital
- Institutional investors seeking 10x+ returns
- Series A: $2–15M | Series B: $15–50M | Series C+: $50M+
- VCs need big outcomes — they're looking for $1B+ companies
- Dilution: each round typically 15–25% dilution

### Revenue-Based Financing
- Repay a percentage of monthly revenue until 1.5–3x is repaid
- No equity dilution
- Good for businesses with predictable revenue

### Small Business Loans
- SBA 7(a): Up to $5M, government-backed, lower rates
- SBA 504: Real estate and equipment, up to $5.5M
- USDA Business Loans: For rural businesses
- Bank term loans, lines of credit, equipment financing

## Exit Strategies

| Exit Type | Description | Multiple |
|-----------|-------------|---------|
| Strategic acquisition | Bought by competitor/larger co. | 4–12x revenue |
| Financial acquisition | Bought by PE/search fund | 3–8x EBITDA |
| IPO | Public offering | Market-determined |
| Management buyout | Team buys the company | Negotiated |
| Merger | Combine with competitor | Negotiated |

**Valuation multiples vary by:**
- Industry (software commands highest multiples)
- Growth rate
- Profitability
- Revenue quality (recurring vs. one-time)
