# Law — Expert Knowledge Base

## Overview of Legal Systems

### Common Law (Anglo-American)
- Law developed through court decisions (judicial precedent / stare decisis)
- Used in: US, UK, Canada, Australia, India
- Precedent from higher courts is binding on lower courts
- Flexible — evolves through interpretation

### Civil Law (Continental)
- Based on comprehensive legal codes (Roman law tradition)
- Used in: France, Germany, Spain, Latin America, Japan
- Judges apply codified statutes; less discretion
- Clear, predictable, systematic

### Religious Law
- **Sharia**: Islamic law derived from Quran, Hadith, and scholarly interpretation
- **Halakha**: Jewish law from the Torah and Talmud
- Governs personal status (marriage, inheritance) in many countries; complete legal system in some (Iran, Saudi Arabia)

### Mixed Systems
- Louisiana (US), Quebec (Canada), South Africa: civil law + common law hybrid
- Scotland: Scots law (Roman + common law)

## Structure of US Law

### Constitutional Layer
- US Constitution: supreme law of the land
- Bill of Rights (Amendments 1–10): individual freedoms
- 14th Amendment: equal protection and due process
- All laws must conform to constitutional limits

### Federal Law
- Acts of Congress: statutory law
- Federal regulations: administrative law (agencies like IRS, SEC, EPA)
- Federal case law: circuit courts → Supreme Court

### State Law
- States have broad police powers (health, safety, morals, welfare)
- State constitutions, statutes, regulations, and case law
- In conflicts with federal law: Supremacy Clause → federal law wins

### Local Law
- County and municipal ordinances
- Zoning, building codes, local business regulations

## Major Branches of Law

### Criminal Law
- The state vs. the defendant
- Burden of proof: **Beyond a reasonable doubt** (~95%+ certainty)
- Felonies (serious crimes) vs. misdemeanors (lesser offenses)
- Elements: actus reus (guilty act) + mens rea (guilty mind) + causation

**Constitutional Protections:**
- 4th Amendment: No unreasonable searches and seizures
- 5th Amendment: No self-incrimination; no double jeopardy; due process
- 6th Amendment: Right to speedy trial, jury, confront witnesses, counsel
- 8th Amendment: No cruel and unusual punishment

### Civil Law (Private Disputes)
- Plaintiff vs. defendant
- Burden of proof: **Preponderance of evidence** (51%+)
- Remedies: monetary damages, injunctions, specific performance

**Major areas:**
- Tort law (negligence, intentional torts, strict liability)
- Contract law
- Property law
- Family law (divorce, custody, inheritance)

### Contract Law
- Elements of a valid contract: offer, acceptance, consideration, capacity, legality
- **UCC** (Uniform Commercial Code): governs sale of goods
- Common Contracts: employment, lease, NDA, LLC operating agreements, purchase agreements
- Breach remedies: compensatory, consequential, punitive, nominal damages

### Tort Law
- **Negligence**: Duty + Breach + Causation + Damages
- **Intentional torts**: assault, battery, false imprisonment, defamation, fraud
- **Strict liability**: inherently dangerous activities (no fault required)
- **Products liability**: manufacturer liable for defective products

### Property Law
- **Real property**: land and structures
- **Personal property**: movable assets
- **Intellectual property**: patents, copyrights, trademarks, trade secrets
- **Adverse possession**: occupy land openly for statutory period → can gain title
- **Eminent domain**: government can take private property for public use (with just compensation)

### Constitutional Law
- Judicial review (Marbury v. Madison, 1803): courts can invalidate unconstitutional laws
- Due process (substantive and procedural)
- Equal protection
- First Amendment: speech, religion, press, assembly, petition

## Intellectual Property Law

### Patents
- Grants exclusive rights to an invention for 20 years from filing
- Types: utility (function), design (appearance), plant (organisms)
- Must be novel, non-obvious, and useful
- Trade secret alternative: indefinite protection if kept secret (Coca-Cola formula)

### Copyright
- Protects original creative works automatically upon creation
- Lasts: author's life + 70 years (US)
- Fair use doctrine: criticism, commentary, parody, education may use without permission

### Trademarks
- Protects brand identifiers (names, logos, slogans)
- Indefinite duration if actively used and defended
- Must be registered with USPTO for maximum protection
- "Naked licensing" (failure to control quality) can result in trademark loss

### Trade Secrets
- Protected indefinitely as long as kept secret
- Coca-Cola formula, Google's PageRank, KFC recipe
- DTSA (Defend Trade Secrets Act): federal protection since 2016

## Business Law Essentials

### Business Formation
- Articles of incorporation/organization filed with state
- Operating agreement (LLC) / bylaws (corporation) govern internal structure
- EIN required for taxes and banking
- Registered agent required for service of process

### Contract Essentials for Business
- Always get it in writing
- Integration clause: written contract supersedes all prior agreements
- Limitation of liability clauses: cap damages
- Arbitration clauses: keep disputes out of court
- Choice of law/forum clauses: select favorable jurisdiction

### Employment Law
- At-will employment (US): either party can terminate at any time for any legal reason
- Protected classes: race, color, religion, sex, national origin, age (40+), disability, pregnancy
- Non-compete agreements: enforceable in most states (California BANS them)
- FLSA: minimum wage, overtime for non-exempt employees
- NLRA: protects workers' right to organize

### Securities Law
- Securities Act of 1933: registration of securities offerings
- Securities Exchange Act of 1934: ongoing reporting + prohibits fraud
- SEC Rule 10b-5: anti-fraud in securities trading (insider trading prosecuted under this)
- Accredited investor: $1M net worth or $200K+ income — access to private placements

## Understanding Legal Processes

### Civil Litigation Timeline
1. Complaint filed → Defendant served
2. Answer filed (20–30 days)
3. Discovery (months to years): depositions, document requests, interrogatories
4. Motions practice (summary judgment, dismissal)
5. Mediation/settlement (90%+ of cases settle)
6. Trial
7. Post-trial motions
8. Appeal (if applicable)

### Criminal Process
1. Arrest → Booking
2. Initial appearance → Bail
3. Arraignment → Plea
4. Preliminary hearing / grand jury
5. Pretrial motions (suppression, dismissal)
6. Plea negotiation (90%+ resolve by plea)
7. Trial
8. Sentencing
9. Appeal

## Key Legal Concepts

### Jurisdiction
- Personal jurisdiction: court's power over the parties
- Subject matter jurisdiction: court's power over the type of case
- In personam (over person) vs. in rem (over property)

### Statute of Limitations
- Time limit to file a claim
- Purpose: preserve evidence quality; provide certainty
- Varies by claim type and jurisdiction
- Discovery rule: clock starts when you knew (or should have known) of harm

### Standing
- Must have suffered concrete harm to bring a lawsuit
- Prevents advisory opinions and hypothetical disputes

### Burden of Proof
| Standard | Used In | Certainty |
|----------|---------|-----------|
| Beyond reasonable doubt | Criminal | ~95% |
| Clear and convincing | Some civil, fraud | ~75% |
| Preponderance of evidence | Most civil | 51%+ |
| Probable cause | Arrests/warrants | Substantial chance |

### Hearsay
- Out-of-court statement offered to prove truth of the matter asserted
- Generally inadmissible (FRE 802)
- Exceptions: admissions, dying declarations, business records, excited utterances, etc.

## Asset Protection Law

### Homestead Exemption
- Florida and Texas: unlimited homestead exemption — primary home untouchable in bankruptcy
- Other states have limits ($25K–$500K depending on state)

### Retirement Accounts
- 401(k), IRA assets: protected from creditors in bankruptcy under ERISA
- IRAs have limits ($1.5M federal); 401(k)s have unlimited federal protection

### Trust Structures
- **Domestic Asset Protection Trust (DAPT)**: irrevocable trust in Nevada, Delaware, South Dakota; assets protected from future creditors after seasoning period
- **Offshore Trusts**: Cook Islands, Nevis trusts — strongest asset protection globally
- **Spendthrift Trusts**: trustee controls distributions; beneficiary's creditors can't reach trust assets

### LLC Charging Order Protection
- Creditors of LLC member can only obtain a "charging order" — rights to distributions
- Cannot force liquidation or management control in most states
- Nevada, Wyoming, Delaware: strongest LLC protections

## International Law

### Treaties and Conventions
- Binding agreements between sovereign states
- Self-executing treaties (domestic law automatically) vs. non-self-executing (require implementing legislation)
- Vienna Convention on the Law of Treaties: governs treaty interpretation

### UNCLOS (Law of the Sea)
- Territorial sea: 12 nautical miles from coast (state sovereignty)
- EEZ (Exclusive Economic Zone): 200 nautical miles — resource rights
- High seas: international waters — freedom of navigation

### Extradition
- Treaties govern surrender of fugitives between countries
- Dual criminality required: crime must be offense in both countries
- Political offense exception (controversial)
- No extradition: Russia, China, many others — no treaties with US for most offenses
