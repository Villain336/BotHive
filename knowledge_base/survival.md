# Survival — Expert Knowledge Base

## The Survival Priority Order

In any survival situation, prioritize in this order:

1. **Protection from immediate threats** (fire, hostile humans, immediate weather)
2. **Shelter** (hypothermia kills faster than anything — within hours in cold)
3. **Signaling** (if rescue is possible, this should be early priority)
4. **Water** (3 days without water; dehydration degrades judgment rapidly)
5. **Food** (3 weeks without food; far less urgent than water)

> "The Rule of 3s": 3 minutes without air, 3 hours without shelter (in extreme cold), 3 days without water, 3 weeks without food.

## Mindset: The Will to Survive

Research on survival situations consistently shows that mental attitude is the primary determinant of survival:
- **STOP**: Stop, Think, Observe, Plan — don't panic; don't move without thinking
- **Positive mental attitude**: Survivors maintain hope and purpose
- **Adaptability**: Rigid thinking kills; creative problem-solving saves lives
- **Viktor Frankl**: Finding meaning in the worst conditions is the deepest survival skill
- The "Rule of thirds": In disasters, ~10% do the right thing, ~80% do nothing (stunned), ~10% do the wrong thing

## Shelter

### Priorities
- Get out of wind, rain, and cold
- Insulation from the ground is more critical than overhead cover (ground conducts heat away rapidly)
- Small shelters retain body heat better

### Shelter Types

**Debris Hut**
- Pile of leaves/debris over a frame
- Must be 1–2 feet thick for insulation
- Size: just big enough to fit your body
- Extremely effective with just natural materials

**Lean-to**
- Single sloped wall against prevailing wind
- Good in mild weather; poor in cold
- Easy and fast to build

**Snow Shelters**
- Quinzhee: pile snow, let sinter 2 hours, hollow out (12 cm walls)
- Snow trench: quick emergency; lay over it with branches and snow
- Igloo: expert level; most effective cold shelter

**Tarp Configurations**
- A-frame, lean-to, plow point (wind deflector)
- Modern survival gear: carry a 8×10 ft tarp minimum

## Water

### Dehydration Effects
- 1% loss: thirst
- 2% loss: reduced cognitive performance
- 5% loss: significant impairment
- 10%+ loss: dangerous; 15–25% can be fatal

### Finding Water
- **Moving water**: Streams and rivers (contaminated but treatable)
- **Rainwater collection**: Tarps, leaves, hollow trees
- **Morning dew**: Collect with absorbent cloth before sunrise
- **Solar still**: Clear plastic over a hole with vegetation; 0.5–1 cup/day (last resort)
- **Plant transpiration bag**: Clear bag over leafy branch; collects moisture overnight
- **Digging for water**: In dry riverbeds, outside of bends (water flows there)

### Water Purification
**Boiling**: Most reliable; 1 minute rolling boil (3 minutes at high altitude) kills pathogens

**Chemical Treatment**:
- Iodine tablets: effective against bacteria and protozoa; not cryptosporidium
- Chlorine dioxide tablets (Aquatabs): effective against all pathogens including crypto; takes 30 min–4 hrs

**Filtration**:
- Sawyer Squeeze/LifeStraw: removes bacteria and protozoa (not viruses)
- MSR Guardian: removes bacteria, protozoa, AND viruses

**UV Treatment**:
- SteriPen: UV light kills all pathogens; requires clear water; battery-dependent

**Improvised filtration**:
- Layers from top to bottom: grass → sand → charcoal → fine sand → gravel
- Removes sediment and improves taste; does NOT sterilize — must boil after

## Fire

### Why Fire
- Warmth (the #1 survival need in cold environments)
- Water purification
- Signaling (smoke visible for miles)
- Cooking (reduces food illness)
- Morale (psychological value is immense)
- Protection from insects and some animals

### Fire Triangle: Oxygen + Heat + Fuel
- **Tinder**: Catches spark easily (dry grass, cattail fluff, birch bark, dry pine needles, char cloth)
- **Kindling**: Small sticks; thumb-sized
- **Fuel**: Logs; hardwood burns slower and hotter

### Primitive Fire Methods

**Bow Drill**
- The most reliable primitive method
- Components: fireboard, spindle, bow, handhold, tinder bundle
- Creates ember through friction; takes practice (failure rate high for beginners)
- Best woods: cottonwood, willow, cedar, basswood (both pieces same or compatible wood)

**Flint and Steel**
- Strike high-carbon steel against flint/chert/quartzite
- Sparks land in char cloth or fungus; blow into tinder bundle

**Hand Drill**
- Harder than bow drill; no cordage needed
- Best in dry conditions; very hard in humidity

### Modern Methods (Carry These)
- **Lighter**: Bic lighter; works wet if dried; 3,000 ignitions
- **Ferrocerium (ferro) rod**: Works wet; 10,000–30,000 strikes; produces 3,000°F sparks
- **Waterproof matches**: Backup
- Principle: carry 3 fire-starting methods

## Food

### Foraging Principles
- **"When in doubt, don't"**: Poisonous mushrooms can be deadly; misidentification kills
- Universal edibility test: Test one part of one plant at a time over 24 hours
- Learn your region's plants (varies enormously by geography)

### High-Value Wild Foods
- **Cattail** (Typha): found near water; every part edible at different seasons
- **Acorns**: High calorie; must leach tannins (soak/boil in multiple changes of water)
- **Dandelion**: Entirely edible; leaves, flowers, root
- **Pine**: Inner bark (cambium), needles (vitamin C tea), seeds (pine nuts)
- **Berries**: Rule — avoid white berries; aggregate berries (blackberry, raspberry) are generally safe

### Hunting and Trapping
- **Primitive traps** (passive hunting — more efficient than active):
  - Figure-4 deadfall: gravity trap for small animals
  - Paiute deadfall: variation of figure-4; easier to set sensitively
  - Snare: wire noose on animal trail; best for rabbits
  - Fish traps: woven basket or rock weir in stream
- **Spears**: Fire-hardened tip; effective in water for fish
- **Rabbit sticks**: thrown projectile for small game

### Insects as Food
- High protein, fat, and micronutrients
- Avoid brightly colored insects (aposematism = warning coloration)
- Cook all insects to kill parasites
- Termites, ants (remove abdomen on fire ants), grasshoppers/crickets, mealworms
- Avoid adult butterflies/moths (inedible); eat caterpillars of non-hairy varieties

## Navigation

### Natural Navigation (No Compass)

**Sun:**
- Northern hemisphere: sun rises northeast (summer), southeast (winter); always in southern sky at noon
- Shadow stick: Mark end of shadow; wait 10–15 minutes; second shadow; line between = E-W; first point = West

**Stars:**
- Polaris (North Star): Find the Big Dipper; line from outer edge of bowl → Polaris = True North
- Southern Cross (Southern hemisphere): Points toward south celestial pole
- Stars rise in the East; set in the West in all locations on Earth

**Compass from Needle:**
- Magnetize needle by stroking against silk/hair in one direction
- Float on leaf in water; points north-south (determine which with sun/stars)

**Terrain:**
- Water flows downhill → follow water to human habitation
- North-facing slopes in northern hemisphere: cooler, moister, more vegetation
- South-facing slopes: drier, warmer, more open

### Map and Compass
- **Declination**: Difference between True North and Magnetic North; must be corrected
- **Taking a bearing**: Point compass at target; rotate until needle aligns with orienting arrow; read bearing
- **Dead reckoning**: Track distance and direction traveled; estimate position

## Medical (Wilderness First Aid)

### The ABCs
- **A**irway: Is it open? Tilt head, lift chin; jaw thrust if spinal injury suspected
- **B**reathing: Is the person breathing?
- **C**irculation: Pulse present? Control severe bleeding first (before anything else)

### Bleeding Control
- Direct pressure: firm, continuous for 10 minutes minimum
- Tourniquet: life-threatening limb bleeding; above wound; note time applied
- Wound packing: for deep wounds that can't be tourniqueted (groin, armpit, neck)

### Hypothermia
- Warning signs: shivering, confusion, slurred speech, stumbling
- Treatment: Remove wet clothing; insulate from ground; warm core first (armpits, groin, neck)
- Do NOT rub extremities (dangerous rewarming)
- Give warm fluids only if conscious

### Dehydration and Heat Stroke
- Heat exhaustion: cool, pale skin; nausea; headache; rest + shade + water
- Heat stroke: hot, red skin; confusion; NO SWEATING = emergency; cool immediately (ice water immersion if available); highest priority casualty

### Wound Care
- Irrigate with clean water under pressure (syringe or water bottle with pinhole)
- Do NOT close dirty wounds — allow to heal open from inside out
- Infection signs: redness spreading, warmth, pus, red streaks (streaks = blood poisoning = emergency)

## Long-Term Survival / Homesteading Overlap

- **Food preservation**: Smoking, salting, drying, fermentation, canning
- **Water systems**: Gravity-fed spring collection, well drilling, rainwater harvesting
- **Seed saving**: Open-pollinated varieties; save for next planting season
- **Energy**: Solar, wood gasification, hand tools
- **Community**: No lone wolf survives long-term; tribe/community is the ultimate survival tool
