# Strategy — Expert Knowledge Base

## What Is Strategy?

Strategy is the art of achieving long-term goals through the optimal deployment of limited resources in the face of opposition or constraints. It exists at the intersection of ends (what you want), ways (how you'll get there), and means (what you have available).

> "Strategy without tactics is the slowest route to victory. Tactics without strategy is the noise before defeat." — Sun Tzu

---

## The Strategic Triangle

```
         ENDS
        (Goals)
         /  \
        /    \
     WAYS --- MEANS
  (Methods)  (Resources)
```

All strategic failure results from misalignment of these three elements:
- **Overextension**: More ends than means can support
- **Inefficiency**: Wrong ways for available means
- **Timidity**: Insufficient ends given available means

## Classical Strategy Frameworks

### Sun Tzu (The Art of War, ~500 BC)
Core principles:
- **Know yourself and know the enemy**: Victory requires intelligence on both
- **Deception is the foundation of war**: All warfare is based on deception
- **Win without fighting**: The supreme excellence is to subdue the enemy without fighting
- **Shape the battlefield before battle**: Position yourself so victory is inevitable
- **Speed**: Strike swiftly, don't allow the enemy to prepare
- **Adaptability**: "Be water" — take the form that the situation requires

Key quotes:
- "All warfare is based on deception"
- "Supreme excellence consists in breaking the enemy's resistance without fighting"
- "The general who wins the battle makes many calculations in his temple before the battle is fought"

### Clausewitz (On War, 1832)
- **War as policy**: "War is the continuation of politics by other means"
- **The Trinity**: War involves the people (passion), the military (chance/creativity), and the government (reason/policy)
- **Friction**: The real world resists perfect execution; account for it
- **The fog of war**: Uncertainty pervades military operations
- **Center of gravity**: The source of an enemy's power; defeat it and the enemy collapses
- **Culminating point of the offensive**: Overextension leads to reversal — know when to stop

### Machiavelli (The Prince, 1513)
- **Realism over idealism**: Deal with the world as it is, not as you wish it to be
- **The ends justify the means** (when necessary): A prince must know how to use the beast and the man
- **Fortune and Virtue**: Success = 50% fortune + 50% virtù (ability, skill, will)
- **Never be neutral**: Taking no side makes you enemy to all
- **Make friends of necessity**: Build coalitions before you need them

## Grand Strategy

Grand strategy coordinates all instruments of power toward national or organizational objectives:
- **Diplomatic**: Alliances, treaties, international organizations
- **Information**: Propaganda, narrative control, intelligence
- **Military**: Coercive and deterrent capabilities
- **Economic**: Sanctions, trade, financial instruments

### Historical Grand Strategies
- **Roman Limes strategy**: Fortified borders + client states as buffers + interior lines of communication
- **British balance of power**: Prevent any single power from dominating the European continent — shift support to maintain equilibrium
- **US containment** (Kennan, 1946): Prevent Soviet expansion without direct war; succeeded 1989
- **Chinese salami slicing**: Incrementally claim territory in small enough steps to not trigger decisive response (South China Sea)

## Competitive Strategy (Business)

### Porter's Five Forces
1. **Threat of new entrants**: High barriers = lower threat = higher profitability
2. **Bargaining power of suppliers**: Few suppliers = high power = lower margins
3. **Bargaining power of buyers**: Few buyers or undifferentiated product = buyer power
4. **Threat of substitutes**: Alternative products cap pricing power
5. **Competitive rivalry**: Intense competition reduces industry profitability

### Generic Competitive Strategies (Porter)
| Strategy | Mechanism | Example |
|----------|-----------|---------|
| Cost Leadership | Be the lowest-cost producer | Walmart, McDonald's |
| Differentiation | Be unique in ways buyers value | Apple, BMW |
| Focus | Serve a narrow segment excellently | Rolls Royce, niche SaaS |

### Blue Ocean Strategy (Kim & Mauborgne)
- Don't compete in existing markets (red ocean) — create uncontested market space (blue ocean)
- Simultaneously reduce costs AND increase value
- **ERRC Framework**: Eliminate, Reduce, Raise, Create (factors vs. competitors)
- Examples: Cirque du Soleil (eliminated animals, added narrative); Nintendo Wii (eliminated graphics power, added motion control)

### Jobs to Be Done (Clayton Christensen)
- Customers don't buy products — they "hire" them to do a job
- Understand the job, not the demographics
- McDonald's milkshakes are "hired" for boring morning commutes

## Game Theory

### Nash Equilibrium
- A state where no player can improve their outcome by unilaterally changing strategy
- Prisoner's Dilemma: individual rationality → collective suboptimality
- Arms races, price wars, and tragedy of the commons are Nash equilibria

### Dominant Strategies
- A strategy that is optimal regardless of what opponents do
- If you have a dominant strategy, play it
- If your opponent has one, assume they'll play it

### Repeated Games
- One-shot prisoner's dilemma → defect
- Repeated prisoner's dilemma → cooperation can emerge
- Tit-for-tat: cooperate first, then mirror opponent's last move — most robust strategy in repeated games

### Signaling
- Costly signals are credible (degree from expensive university signals ability to succeed there)
- Cheap talk is unreliable (anyone can claim quality)
- Strategic credibility through commitment mechanisms (burning bridges = credible commitment)

### Schelling Points
- Focal points where people coordinate without communication
- If told to meet someone in New York tomorrow at noon, most people choose the Grand Central clock
- Borders, round numbers, and prominent landmarks serve as coordination devices in negotiations

## Strategic Planning Process

### SWOT Analysis
- **Strengths**: Internal advantages
- **Weaknesses**: Internal disadvantages
- **Opportunities**: External factors to exploit
- **Threats**: External factors to mitigate

### OKRs (Objectives and Key Results)
- **Objective**: Qualitative, inspiring direction ("Become the market leader in SMB accounting software")
- **Key Results**: Quantitative, measurable outcomes ("Acquire 10,000 new paying customers"; "Achieve NPS > 50")
- Quarterly cadence; 60–70% achievement is ideal (too easy = not ambitious enough)

### The Balanced Scorecard (Kaplan & Norton)
Four perspectives:
1. Financial: How do we look to shareholders?
2. Customer: How do customers see us?
3. Internal Processes: What must we excel at?
4. Learning and Growth: Can we continue to improve?

## Negotiation Strategy

### BATNA (Best Alternative to a Negotiated Agreement)
- The most important concept in negotiation
- Know your BATNA; improve it before negotiating
- Never accept a deal worse than your BATNA
- Attack the opponent's BATNA (make their alternatives worse)

### Principled Negotiation (Getting to Yes)
- Separate people from problems
- Focus on interests, not positions
- Generate options for mutual gain
- Use objective criteria

### Distributive vs. Integrative Bargaining
- **Distributive**: Fixed pie; your gain = their loss (zero-sum). Tactics: anchoring, false deadlines, bogey
- **Integrative**: Expand the pie through creative trades on different priorities. Both parties can win more.

### Anchoring
- First number in any negotiation becomes the reference point
- Aggressive anchors pull outcomes toward the anchor
- Counter-anchor or reject the frame entirely

## Influence and Persuasion (Cialdini)

1. **Reciprocity**: We feel obligated to return favors — give first to receive
2. **Commitment and Consistency**: We justify previous commitments — get small yeses first
3. **Social Proof**: We look to others' behavior in uncertain situations — testimonials, crowd
4. **Authority**: We comply with experts and symbols of authority
5. **Liking**: We say yes to people we like — rapport, similarity, flattery
6. **Scarcity**: We value what is rare or dwindling — limited time, limited availability
7. **Unity** (added later): We comply with in-group members — us vs. them identity

## Strategic Deception

### Military Deception (MILDEC)
- **Feints**: Simulate attack at one point to draw forces from the real target
- **Disinformation**: Feed false intelligence to the enemy
- **D-Day deception (Operation Fortitude)**: Fake army group under Patton convinced Germans the real landing would be at Pas-de-Calais

### Red Herring and Misdirection
- Distract attention from the real move
- Magicians, politicians, and generals all use this

### The 36 Chinese Stratagems
Ancient military strategies still applicable:
- **Stratagem 1**: Cross the sea without the sky's knowledge (conceal your intentions in plain sight)
- **Stratagem 13**: Beat the grass to startle the snake (probe before committing)
- **Stratagem 20**: Let the plum tree wither in place of the peach (sacrifice small things for large ones)
- **Stratagem 36**: If all else fails, retreat (the ability to disengage is itself a power)

## Long-Term Strategy

### Second and Third-Order Effects
- First order: the immediate, obvious consequence of an action
- Second order: the consequence of the consequence
- Third order: the consequence of that
- Strategic thinkers reason several moves ahead and account for emergent effects

### Reversibility
- Prefer reversible decisions when uncertain; irreversible decisions deserve more deliberation
- Bezos's "two-way door vs. one-way door" framework

### Optionality
- Keep options open; avoid premature commitment
- Real options thinking: the value of a decision includes the value of future options it creates or destroys

### The Overton Window
- The range of politically acceptable positions at any given time
- Shift the Overton Window first; then policy change becomes possible
- Think tanks, media, and extremists all move the window
