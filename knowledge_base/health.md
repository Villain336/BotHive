## Sleep

### Why Sleep Is Non-Negotiable
- Glymphatic system: during deep sleep, cerebrospinal fluid flushes toxic proteins (amyloid-beta, tau) from the brain — the mechanism of Alzheimer's prevention
- Emotional processing: REM sleep consolidates emotional memories and reduces emotional reactivity
- Hormone production: 95% of human growth hormone released during deep sleep
- Immune function: NK cell activity drops 70% after one night of 4-hour sleep
- Memory consolidation: hippocampus transfers short-term memories to long-term storage

### Sleep Architecture
- 90-minute sleep cycles, 4–6 per night
- **NREM Stage 1/2**: Light sleep; easy to wake
- **NREM Stage 3/4 (Slow Wave Sleep)**: Physical restoration; growth hormone; hardest to wake
- **REM**: Dreaming; emotional processing; creativity; learning consolidation

### Optimizing Sleep
- **Temperature**: Core body temp must drop 1–3°F to initiate sleep; keep bedroom 65–68°F
- **Light**: Block blue light 1–2 hours before bed; complete darkness during sleep (blackout curtains)
- **Schedule**: Consistent wake time is more important than consistent bedtime
- **Alcohol**: Disrupts REM sleep; causes fragmented sleep in second half of night
- **Caffeine**: 5–7 hour half-life; stop by 12–2 PM for most people
- **Melatonin**: 0.5–3 mg taken 30–60 minutes before desired sleep time (not a sleep inducer — a timing signal)

### Sleep Disorders
- **Insomnia**: Difficulty falling or staying asleep; CBT-I (cognitive behavioral therapy for insomnia) is first-line treatment
- **Sleep apnea**: Airway obstruction causes breathing stops; CPAP therapy; weight loss
- **Restless leg syndrome**: Uncomfortable sensations causing urge to move; iron supplementation, dopamine medications

## Nutrition

### Macronutrients

**Protein (4 cal/g)**
- Most important macronutrient for body composition
- Target: 0.7–1.0g per pound of body weight daily (higher if strength training)
- Complete proteins: contain all 9 essential amino acids
- Best sources: eggs, chicken breast, fish, beef, Greek yogurt, cottage cheese
- Leucine content triggers muscle protein synthesis — ~2.5g leucine per meal needed

**Fats (9 cal/g)**
- Saturated fat: meat, dairy — not as harmful as previously believed
- Monounsaturated fat (MUFA): olive oil, avocado, almonds — cardioprotective
- Polyunsaturated fat: omega-3 (fish, flaxseed) + omega-6 (vegetable oils)
- Trans fat (partially hydrogenated oils): clearly harmful; avoid entirely
- Omega-3:6 ratio: target 1:4 or better; Western diet is typically 1:20–1:50 (inflammatory)

**Carbohydrates (4 cal/g)**
- Primary fuel source for brain and high-intensity exercise
- Quality matters: whole grains, legumes, vegetables vs. refined sugars
- Glycemic index/load: rate and magnitude of blood sugar spike
- Fiber: 25–38g daily recommended; feeds gut microbiome; reduces cardiovascular risk

### Micronutrients (High-Impact Deficiencies)

| Nutrient | Deficiency Effects | Sources |
|----------|-------------------|---------|
| Vitamin D | Immune dysfunction, depression, bone loss, cancer risk | Sun, fatty fish, fortified foods; supplement 2,000–5,000 IU |
| Magnesium | Poor sleep, muscle cramps, insulin resistance, anxiety | Leafy greens, nuts, seeds, chocolate |
| Omega-3 (EPA/DHA) | Inflammation, depression, cognitive decline | Fatty fish; supplement 1–3g daily |
| B12 | Anemia, neurological damage | Animal products; critical for vegans to supplement |
| Iron | Anemia, fatigue, poor cognition | Red meat, legumes; absorption enhanced by vitamin C |
| Zinc | Immune dysfunction, testosterone drop, wound healing | Oysters, red meat, pumpkin seeds |
| Iodine | Thyroid dysfunction, cognitive impairment | Iodized salt, seafood |

### Dietary Frameworks

**Mediterranean Diet**
- Strong evidence base for cardiovascular health and longevity
- High in: olive oil, fish, vegetables, legumes, whole grains, nuts
- Moderate: wine
- Low: red meat, processed foods

**Whole Food Plant-Based (WFPB)**
- Maximum evidence for reversing heart disease (Ornish, Esselstyn)
- Lowest all-cause mortality in Seventh-Day Adventist studies (Loma Linda, CA)
- Challenges: protein quality, B12, iron, zinc, omega-3

**Ketogenic Diet**
- Very low carb (<20–50g/day); high fat; moderate protein
- Forces liver to produce ketone bodies as brain/body fuel
- Benefits: weight loss, blood sugar control, epilepsy management
- Risks: electrolyte imbalances, constipation, cholesterol changes

**Intermittent Fasting**
- 16:8 (16hr fast, 8hr eating window)
- Benefits: autophagy, insulin sensitivity, caloric restriction without counting
- 72-hour fasts: significant autophagy and immune system reset

**Caloric Restriction**
- Single most reproducible longevity intervention in every organism studied
- 15–30% caloric restriction extends lifespan up to 30–40% in animal models
- CALERIE trial: 25% restriction improved biomarkers of aging in humans

### The Gut Microbiome
- ~38 trillion bacteria in the gut; 1.5–3 kg of microbial weight
- Produces neurotransmitters (90% of serotonin produced in gut)
- Gut-brain axis: bidirectional communication; gut health affects mood, cognition, immunity
- Diversity is health: eat 30+ different plant foods per week
- Prebiotics: feed bacteria (fiber, resistant starch, inulin)
- Probiotics: live bacteria (fermented foods: kefir, kimchi, sauerkraut, yogurt)
- Antibiotics: devastating to microbiome; avoid unless medically necessary

## Exercise

### Why Exercise Is the Ultimate Longevity Drug
- Reduces all-cause mortality by 35%
- Reduces cardiovascular disease risk by 40–50%
- Reduces cancer risk by 30–40%
- Reduces dementia risk by 30–40%
- Equivalent to or superior to antidepressants for mild-moderate depression

### Zone 2 Cardio (Aerobic Base)
- Low intensity steady-state where you can hold a conversation
- Primarily fuels mitochondria using fat oxidation
- Target: 150–180 minutes per week minimum
- Builds mitochondrial density — the cellular energy factories
- Improves insulin sensitivity; reduces metabolic disease
- VO2 max is the single best predictor of all-cause mortality

### High-Intensity Interval Training (HIIT)
- Brief bursts at maximum effort followed by rest
- Raises VO2 max more effectively than Zone 2
- Norwegian 4×4 protocol: 4 min at 90%+ max HR × 4 rounds; rest 3 min between
- 2× per week optimal for most; more does not equal more benefit

### Strength Training
- Critical for longevity: muscle mass and grip strength are strong predictors of longevity
- Stimulates release of myokines — anti-inflammatory hormones from muscle tissue
- Prevents sarcopenia (age-related muscle loss)
- Supports bone density (prevents osteoporosis)
- Improves insulin sensitivity and glucose uptake
- 2–4× per week; major compound movements: squat, deadlift, bench, row, overhead press

### VO2 Max
- Maximum oxygen the body can utilize during exercise
- Elite athletes: 70–85 ml/kg/min; average person: 35–45
- Going from "low" to "above average" VO2 max reduces all-cause mortality by 50%

## Longevity Science

### The Hallmarks of Aging (Lopez-Otin, 2013 — updated 2023)
1. Genomic instability (DNA damage accumulation)
2. Telomere attrition (chromosome end shortening)
3. Epigenetic alterations (gene expression changes)
4. Loss of proteostasis (protein folding/clearing defects)
5. Deregulated nutrient sensing (mTOR, AMPK, insulin pathways)
6. Mitochondrial dysfunction
7. Cellular senescence (zombie cells that secrete inflammatory signals)
8. Stem cell exhaustion
9. Altered intercellular communication (chronic inflammation — "inflammaging")
10. Disabled macroautophagy
11. Dysbiosis (gut microbiome disruption)
12. Chronic inflammation

### Longevity Interventions

| Intervention | Mechanism | Evidence |
|-------------|-----------|---------|
| Caloric restriction | Reduces mTOR, increases AMPK, autophagy | Very strong in animals; good in humans |
| Exercise | Every hallmark improved | Strongest human evidence |
| Rapamycin | mTOR inhibitor; extends lifespan in every organism tested | Strong animal data; early human use |
| Metformin | AMPK activator; insulin sensitizer | Strong epidemiological; TAME trial ongoing |
| NMN/NR (NAD+ precursors) | Restores NAD+ levels (decline with age) | Promising; human trials ongoing |
| Fasting/Autophagy | Cellular cleanup; removes senescent proteins | Strong mechanistic data |
| Senolytics | Eliminate senescent ("zombie") cells | Dasatinib + Quercetin; early human trials |

### Blue Zones (Dan Buettner)
Populations with highest longevity concentration:
- Sardinia, Italy (high centenarian rate, especially men)
- Okinawa, Japan (longest lived women)
- Loma Linda, California (Seventh-Day Adventists)
- Nicoya Peninsula, Costa Rica
- Ikaria, Greece

**Common factors:**
1. Plant-heavy diet; meat eaten occasionally
2. Daily moderate physical activity (walking, gardening)
3. Low stress; purpose (ikigai in Japan; plan de vida in Costa Rica)
4. Social connection; strong family and community bonds
5. Moderate alcohol consumption (mostly wine)
6. Sleep and rest

## Mental Health

### Depression
- Affects 280 million people worldwide
- Neurobiological: low serotonin, norepinephrine, dopamine
- Evidence-based treatments: CBT, SSRI antidepressants, exercise, ketamine (treatment-resistant)
- Lifestyle factors: sleep, social connection, sunlight, exercise are powerful non-pharmacological interventions

### Anxiety
- Most common mental disorder globally
- Treatments: CBT (gold standard), SSRIs/SNRIs, benzodiazepines (short-term only)
- Chronic stress → elevated cortisol → hippocampal damage → learning/memory impairment

### Hormetic Stress (Good Stress)
- Brief, moderate stress that triggers adaptive response:
- Exercise (muscle damage → growth)
- Cold exposure (cold shock proteins, norepinephrine surge)
- Heat exposure (sauna: heat shock proteins, cardiovascular adaptation)
- Intermittent fasting (mild metabolic stress → improved resilience)
