# Execution — Expert Knowledge Base

## What Is Execution?

Execution is the discipline of turning plans into results. Strategy without execution is fiction. Most organizations and individuals fail not because they had bad ideas but because they could not execute. Execution is the bridge between vision and reality.

> "In real life, strategy is actually very straightforward. You pick a general direction and implement it like hell." — Jack Welch

---

## Why Execution Fails

### The Knowing-Doing Gap (Pfeffer & Sutton)
- Organizations know what to do but don't do it
- Causes:
  - Confusion of talk with action (meetings, memos, plans substitute for work)
  - Fear of failure punishes experimentation
  - Internal competition destroys collaboration
  - Measurement issues: can't measure what matters, so measure what's easy

### Common Execution Failures
1. **Fuzzy goals**: Vague objectives ("be better at customer service") vs. specific outcomes ("resolve 95% of tickets in under 4 hours")
2. **Too many priorities**: If everything is a priority, nothing is
3. **Accountability gaps**: Everyone's responsible = nobody's responsible
4. **Lag vs. lead measures**: Measuring outcomes after the fact instead of tracking leading indicators
5. **Wrong people**: Skills/motivation mismatch to the task
6. **Poor sequencing**: Doing tasks in suboptimal order
7. **Under-resourcing**: Insufficient time, money, or attention

## Execution Frameworks

### The 4 Disciplines of Execution (McChesney, Covey, Huling)

**Discipline 1: Focus on the Wildly Important Goal (WIG)**
- 1–2 goals, not 10
- What would make everything else easier or irrelevant?

**Discipline 2: Act on Lead Measures**
- Lag measures: results (sales revenue, weight lost) — can't be directly acted on
- Lead measures: inputs you control that predict lag results (calls made, meals logged)

**Discipline 3: Keep a Compelling Scoreboard**
- People play differently when they're keeping score
- Visual, public, and updated in real time

**Discipline 4: Create a Cadence of Accountability**
- Weekly WIG meeting: What were my commitments? Did I keep them? What are this week's commitments?
- Regular rhythm makes accountability predictable and non-threatening

### OKRs (Objectives and Key Results)
Used by: Google, Intel, LinkedIn, Twitter

**Structure:**
- Objective: Qualitative, inspiring, time-bound (quarterly)
- Key Results: 3–5 specific, measurable outcomes that prove the objective was achieved

**Principles:**
- Set at all levels (company → team → individual)
- Public across the organization
- Aspirational (60–70% achievement = success)
- Separate from compensation (don't game them)

### The EOS (Entrepreneurial Operating System)
Designed for companies of 10–250 people:

**Six Key Components:**
1. **Vision**: Where are we going? (10-year target, 3-year picture, 1-year plan)
2. **People**: Right people in right seats (Core Values fit + GWC: Get it, Want it, Capacity)
3. **Data**: Scorecard of 5–15 weekly metrics; everyone knows the numbers
4. **Issues**: IDS — Identify, Discuss, Solve — real issues resolved in the room
5. **Process**: Core processes documented and followed by all
6. **Traction**: Rocks (90-day priorities) + L10 meetings (weekly structured meeting)

## Personal Execution

### GTD (Getting Things Done — David Allen)
The system:
1. **Capture**: Collect everything that has your attention into an inbox
2. **Clarify**: Process each item — is it actionable? If yes, what's the next action?
3. **Organize**: Put it where it belongs (calendar, project list, reference, trash)
4. **Reflect**: Review lists regularly (weekly review is critical)
5. **Engage**: Simply do the work with a clear mind

**Two-minute rule**: If an action takes less than 2 minutes, do it now

### Time Blocking
- Assign specific tasks to specific time slots on your calendar
- Protect deep work blocks (no meetings, no interruptions)
- Batch similar tasks (all calls in one block, all email in one block)
- Buffer time (20% of calendar) for unexpected demands

### The Eisenhower Matrix
| | Urgent | Not Urgent |
|--|--------|------------|
| **Important** | Do immediately | Schedule (Quadrant 2: highest value) |
| **Not Important** | Delegate | Eliminate |

Quadrant 2 (Important + Not Urgent) is where: exercise, relationships, strategy, learning, and system building live. Most people neglect it — and pay the price.

### Deep Work (Cal Newport)
- Cognitively demanding work performed in a state of distraction-free concentration
- The ability to do deep work is becoming increasingly rare and increasingly valuable
- **Shallow work**: Email, admin, meetings — valuable but not transformative
- **Schedule philosophy**: Deep work first; shallow work in remaining time
- **4 hour rule**: Most people can do ~4 hours of true deep work per day; maximize those hours

## Team Execution

### RACI Matrix
Assigns responsibility for every task:
- **R (Responsible)**: Does the work
- **A (Accountable)**: Owns the outcome; the "single throat to choke"
- **C (Consulted)**: Input sought before decisions made
- **I (Informed)**: Kept updated

**Rule**: Each task has exactly ONE A. Multiple A's = no accountability.

### Sprint Methodology (Agile)
- Work in 1–2 week sprints with fixed scope
- Daily stand-up: What did I do yesterday? What will I do today? Any blockers?
- Sprint review: Demo completed work
- Sprint retrospective: What went well? What should change?
- Velocity: Track how much work gets done per sprint; use for forecasting

### The After Action Review (AAR — Military Origin)
After every significant action:
1. What was supposed to happen?
2. What actually happened?
3. Why was there a difference?
4. What do we sustain? What do we improve?

### The Manager's Schedule vs. Maker's Schedule (Paul Graham)
- Managers: 1-hour calendar blocks; can handle many topics
- Makers: Need half-days minimum for creative/deep work
- A single meeting can destroy a maker's productive afternoon
- Protect your makers' calendar from meeting sprawl

## Operational Excellence

### The Toyota Production System (TPS) / Lean
Core concepts:
- **Just-in-time (JIT)**: Produce what's needed, when needed, in the quantity needed
- **Jidoka**: Stop and fix problems immediately; don't pass defects downstream
- **Kaizen**: Continuous improvement — small, daily improvements accumulate
- **5S**: Sort, Straighten, Shine, Standardize, Sustain — workplace organization
- **Value Stream Mapping**: Visualize entire process from input to customer delivery; identify waste (muda)

**8 Forms of Waste (DOWNTIME):**
- Defects, Overproduction, Waiting, Non-Utilized talent, Transportation, Inventory, Motion, Extra processing

### Six Sigma
- Data-driven approach to reducing defects and variation
- **DMAIC**: Define, Measure, Analyze, Improve, Control
- Sigma levels: 6 sigma = 3.4 defects per million opportunities
- Belt levels: White, Yellow, Green, Black, Master Black Belt

## Execution Under Pressure

### Decision-Making Under Uncertainty
- **OODA Loop**: Observe → Orient → Decide → Act (then repeat)
- Speed matters: faster decision cycle defeats slower opponent
- 70% solution now beats 100% solution too late (Colin Powell's rule)

### Crisis Management
- Pre-designate decision authority (don't slow down during crisis with approval chains)
- Establish communication rhythm
- Separate monitoring from action (don't have decision-makers glued to news feeds)
- "First, stabilize" — stop the bleeding before strategizing

### The Murphy Principle
- Anything that can go wrong will go wrong (at the worst possible time)
- Pre-mortem: before launching, assume failure. Why did it fail? Fix those weaknesses now
- Red teaming: assign a team to find every flaw in your plan

## Habits and Routines as Execution Infrastructure

### Habit Loop (Charles Duhigg)
- Cue → Routine → Reward
- To change a habit: keep cue and reward; change the routine

### Keystone Habits
- Some habits unlock other habits (exercise → better sleep → better focus → better eating)
- Identify your keystone habit; install it first

### Morning Routines of High Performers
- Wake early (most high performers wake 5–6 AM)
- No phone for first 30–60 minutes
- Physical movement
- Planning the day on paper
- High-priority work before checking email

## Metrics and Accountability

### What Gets Measured Gets Managed (Drucker)
- Choose metrics carefully — people optimize for what's measured
- Goodhart's Law: When a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure
- Leading vs. lagging: lead indicators predict; lag indicators confirm

### Weekly Review Cadence
- Review all open projects
- Clear inboxes to zero
- Review calendar for upcoming obligations
- Update task lists
- Identify top 3 priorities for the week
