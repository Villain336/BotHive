# Loopholes — Expert Knowledge Base

## Definition

A loophole is a legal ambiguity or omission that allows individuals or organizations to circumvent the intent of a rule while complying with its letter. Loopholes are features, not bugs — they exist because laws are written by humans and no law is perfectly comprehensive.

> **Note**: This knowledge base covers legal loopholes and lawful strategies. Illegal tax evasion, fraud, and other criminal schemes are not covered and are not advised.

---

## Tax Loopholes (Legal)

### 1. The Buy, Borrow, Die Strategy
- **Mechanism**: Buy appreciating assets → borrow against them (loans not taxable) → die and pass to heirs at stepped-up basis
- **Effect**: Avoid capital gains tax indefinitely
- **Used by**: Elon Musk, Jeff Bezos, Larry Ellison

### 2. Stepped-Up Basis at Death
- When you die, your heirs receive assets at the **current** fair market value, not what you paid
- Capital gains accumulated over a lifetime are permanently erased
- A $100K investment that grew to $1M: heirs inherit $1M basis — zero capital gains on $900K gain

### 3. 1031 Exchange (Real Estate)
- Sell a rental property → use proceeds to buy a "like-kind" property
- Capital gains tax deferred indefinitely as long as you keep exchanging
- Can be done repeatedly until death (then stepped-up basis eliminates the gain)

### 4. Opportunity Zone Investments
- Invest capital gains into designated low-income areas (Opportunity Zones)
- Capital gains deferred until 2026 OR when investment is sold
- If held 10+ years: **zero** capital gains tax on new appreciation
- Created by the Tax Cuts and Jobs Act of 2017

### 5. Qualified Small Business Stock (QSBS) — Section 1202
- Invest in a qualifying small C-Corp at founding
- Hold for 5+ years
- Exclude **up to $10 million** (or 10x basis) in capital gains from federal tax
- Some states (California) don't recognize this exclusion

### 6. Backdoor Roth IRA
- Income too high for Roth IRA? Contribute to non-deductible Traditional IRA → immediately convert to Roth
- No income limits on conversion
- Future growth and withdrawals are tax-free

### 7. Mega Backdoor Roth
- Contribute after-tax dollars to 401(k) up to $66,000 (2023 limit)
- Convert to Roth IRA or Roth 401(k)
- Shields up to $43,500 extra from taxes annually (beyond standard 401k limit)

### 8. Health Savings Account (HSA) Triple Tax Advantage
- Contributions are pre-tax
- Growth is tax-free
- Withdrawals for qualified medical expenses are tax-free
- After 65: withdraw for ANY purpose (taxed like Traditional IRA — no penalty)
- Invest HSA funds rather than spending; pay current medical bills out-of-pocket; decades later reimburse yourself tax-free

### 9. Augusta Rule (Section 280A)
- Rent your home to your business for up to **14 days per year** — tax-free income to you
- The business deducts the rent payment
- Named after Augusta, GA (Masters golf tournament week)
- Must charge fair market rate; document properly

### 10. Depreciation (Cost Segregation)
- Real estate investors depreciate properties over 27.5 years (residential) or 39 years (commercial)
- **Cost segregation study**: Accelerate depreciation of certain components (fixtures, landscaping, parking) to 5, 7, or 15 years
- **Bonus depreciation**: 100% first-year deduction on qualifying property (phasing down from 2023)
- Can create massive "paper losses" that offset real income

### 11. Real Estate Professional Status
- Spend 750+ hours/year AND more than 50% of working time in real estate
- Rental losses become **active losses** that offset W-2 and business income
- No income limits on deductions
- Potential to offset $100K+ in income annually

### 12. Deferred Compensation Plans
- Executives can defer unlimited compensation to future years
- Defers taxation until withdrawal (hopefully at lower rate in retirement)
- Risk: it's a company liability — if company goes bankrupt, you lose deferred compensation

### 13. Charitable Remainder Trust (CRT)
- Donate appreciated assets to trust
- Trust sells assets with **no capital gains tax**
- Trust pays you income stream for life
- Remainder passes to charity
- You get charitable deduction at time of contribution

### 14. Donor-Advised Fund (DAF)
- Contribute cash or appreciated assets to DAF — immediate tax deduction
- Assets grow tax-free inside the fund
- Grant to charities over time (no rush)
- Donate stock that has appreciated — avoid capital gains AND get full deduction

### 15. S-Corp Self-Employment Tax Strategy
- Self-employed pay 15.3% SE tax on ALL profits
- With S-Corp election: pay yourself "reasonable salary" (subject to SE tax) + take additional profit as distribution (NOT subject to SE tax)
- Example: $200K net profit → $70K salary + $130K distribution = $10K in SE tax savings

## Business and Corporate Loopholes

### Transfer Pricing
- Multinational corporations charge their international subsidiaries for goods/services
- By setting prices between subsidiaries, profits can be shifted to low-tax jurisdictions
- Apple, Google, and other multinationals use this to book profits in Ireland (12.5% tax) vs. US (21% tax)

### The Delaware Holding Company
- Form a holding company in Delaware
- License your IP, trademarks, or brand to your operating company
- Royalty payments flow to Delaware holding company (no state income tax)
- Used by companies to reduce state tax burden in high-tax states

### Puerto Rico Act 60 (formerly Act 22/20)
- Relocate to Puerto Rico and become a bona fide resident
- **Individual Investors** (Act 22): 0% tax on dividends, interest, and capital gains
- **Export Services** (Act 20): 4% corporate tax rate on export services
- Requirements: Live in PR 183+ days/year; don't maintain US home; Puerto Rican source income

### Captive Insurance Company
- Company creates its own insurance subsidiary
- Pays "premiums" to the captive (tax-deductible)
- Captive invests premium pool
- If no large claims, wealth accumulates in low-tax captive entity
- Must have genuine insurance risk and be properly structured (IRS scrutinizes abusive arrangements)

## Legal Loopholes

### Statute of Limitations
- Criminal and civil claims must be filed within a specified time period
- After the period expires, even a legitimate claim cannot be pursued
- Varies by offense and jurisdiction (e.g., federal tax fraud: 6 years; murder: no SOL)

### Sovereign Immunity
- Governments and their agencies cannot be sued without consent
- Federal Tort Claims Act (FTCA) waives immunity for certain claims
- Foreign sovereigns have immunity under the Foreign Sovereign Immunities Act

### The Foreign Earned Income Exclusion (FEIE)
- US citizens living abroad can exclude up to $126,500 (2024) of foreign earned income from US federal taxes
- Must pass the Physical Presence Test (330 days outside US) or Bona Fide Residence Test
- Does NOT eliminate self-employment tax or investment income tax

### Homestead Exemption
- Florida and Texas have **unlimited** homestead exemption
- Your primary residence is protected from most creditors in bankruptcy
- OJ Simpson and others have used Florida's unlimited exemption to protect assets from civil judgments

### Entity Liability Shields
- Corporations and LLCs shield personal assets from business liabilities
- "Piercing the corporate veil" can eliminate this protection if:
  - Personal and business funds are commingled
  - Formalities are not observed (no minutes, no separate accounts)
  - Entity is fraudulently used

## Immigration and Citizenship Loopholes

### Anchor Baby / Birthright Citizenship
- 14th Amendment: anyone born on US soil is automatically a citizen
- Creates a path to legal residency for the parents

### EB-5 Investor Visa
- Invest $800,000–$1,050,000 in a job-creating US enterprise
- Receive a green card for yourself and immediate family
- Used extensively by foreign nationals to obtain US residency

### Citizenship by Investment
- Dominica: $100,000 donation or $200,000 real estate investment → citizenship
- Malta: €600,000–€750,000 investment → EU citizenship (access to 27 EU countries)
- Portugal Golden Visa: €500,000 real estate → residency → citizenship after 5 years

## Loopholes in Regulation

### The Regulatory Capture Problem
- Industries often lobby to shape the regulations that govern them
- Result: Regulations that appear protective actually favor incumbents and create barriers to entry for new competitors
- Example: Pharmaceutical patent extensions, airline slot controls, banking regulations written by bank lobbyists

### LLC Operating Agreement Flexibility
- LLCs are not bound by statutory rules in most states
- The operating agreement can override default rules
- Create super-voting shares, waterfall distributions, drag-along rights, and virtually any other arrangement

## Key Cautions

- **Tax avoidance** (legal) vs. **tax evasion** (illegal) — the distinction matters enormously
- IRS scrutinizes "listed transactions" and tax shelters; document everything
- Substance over form doctrine: IRS can recharacterize transactions that lack economic substance
- Step transaction doctrine: IRS can collapse a series of steps into one transaction for tax purposes
- Laws change — today's loophole may be tomorrow's closed provision
