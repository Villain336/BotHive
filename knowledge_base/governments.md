# Governments — Expert Knowledge Base

## What Is Government?

Government is the system of institutions and people with the authority to make and enforce rules for a group of people (typically citizens of a nation-state). It holds the monopoly on legitimate use of force within its territory (Max Weber's definition of the state).

---

## The Origin of States

### Social Contract Theory
- **Hobbes**: Without government, life is "solitary, poor, nasty, brutish, and short" — Leviathan needed to impose order
- **Locke**: People surrender some freedom in exchange for protection of life, liberty, and property — government derives power from consent
- **Rousseau**: The "general will" — collective decision-making for common good

### Conquest Theory
- Most states were founded by force — one group conquered another and established rule
- Modern states that trace lineage to peaceful founding are rare exceptions

### Social Order Theory
- States emerge organically from population growth, trade, and the need for coordination
- Legitimacy accumulates over time through habit, culture, and delivered services

## Forms of Government

### Democracy
- **Direct democracy**: Citizens vote directly on laws (ancient Athens, Swiss cantons)
- **Representative democracy**: Elected officials vote on behalf of citizens
- **Liberal democracy**: Includes minority protections, rule of law, and civil liberties

### Republic
- A government in which power is held by elected representatives
- Anti-majoritarian features: constitutions, courts, individual rights
- The US is a constitutional republic (not a pure democracy)

### Authoritarianism
- Concentrated power; limited political freedom; no meaningful accountability
- Stable through surveillance, repression, and propaganda
- Types: military junta, one-party state, personalist dictatorship, religious autocracy

### Totalitarianism
- Extreme authoritarianism: seeks to control every aspect of public and private life
- Examples: Nazi Germany, Stalinist USSR, Mao's China, North Korea
- Requires ideology, mass mobilization, terror apparatus

### Theocracy
- God or divine law governs (through human interpreters)
- Examples: Iran (Islamic Republic), Vatican City (Pope), Saudi Arabia (King + Sharia)

### Oligarchy
- Rule by a small, privileged group (wealthy, military, ethnic)
- Most formal democracies have oligarchic elements (wealth → political influence)
- Russia is an oligarchy with a democratic facade

### Monarchy
- **Absolute monarchy**: King/queen has unlimited power (Saudi Arabia)
- **Constitutional monarchy**: Monarch's powers limited by constitution (UK, Sweden, Japan)
- Hereditary succession creates stability but also incompetence

### Technocracy
- Rule by experts
- European Union has strong technocratic elements (unelected Commission)
- Singapore: semi-technocratic, extremely effective governance
- Arguments for: better outcomes; arguments against: unaccountable to public

### Anarchy
- Absence of formal government
- Not chaos — anarchists envision voluntary cooperation and horizontal organization
- Brief historical examples: Revolutionary Catalonia (1936), free zones in Somalia

## Government Functions

### Core Functions
1. **Security**: Maintain military and police force; protect borders
2. **Justice**: Adjudicate disputes; punish crime
3. **Property rights**: Define and enforce ownership
4. **Contract enforcement**: Enable market exchange by honoring agreements
5. **Public goods**: Provide what markets underserve (defense, infrastructure, lighthouses)

### Extended Functions (varies by system)
- Redistribution: Taxes and transfers to reduce inequality
- Regulation: Environmental, financial, consumer protection
- Social insurance: Unemployment, healthcare, pensions
- Education: Public schooling
- Infrastructure: Roads, bridges, electrical grids, internet

### Where Governments Fail
- **Rent-seeking**: Interest groups capture regulatory agencies (regulatory capture)
- **Public choice problem**: Politicians maximize votes, not social welfare
- **Knowledge problem**: Central planners can't match the information efficiency of markets
- **Principal-agent problem**: Bureaucrats may pursue their own interests, not the public's
- **Corruption**: Self-dealing by officials at the expense of public

## US Government Structure

### The Three Branches
**Legislative (Congress)**:
- Senate: 100 senators (2 per state); 6-year terms; confirms executive appointments, ratifies treaties
- House of Representatives: 435 members; proportional to population; 2-year terms; originates revenue bills
- Powers: pass laws, declare war, control spending (power of the purse), impeach officials

**Executive (President)**:
- Head of state, head of government, commander-in-chief
- Signs or vetoes legislation
- Appoints cabinet, federal judges, ambassadors
- Executes and enforces laws
- Issues executive orders (binding within executive branch)

**Judicial (Courts)**:
- Supreme Court: 9 justices; lifetime appointment; final arbiter of Constitution
- Circuit Courts of Appeals: 13 circuits
- District Courts: 94 districts
- Judicial review: power to invalidate unconstitutional laws

### Federalism
- Power divided between federal and state governments
- 10th Amendment: powers not delegated to federal government reserved to states
- Interstate commerce clause has been broadly interpreted to expand federal power
- Federal supremacy: federal law > state law on the same subject

## How Laws Are Made (US)

1. Bill introduced in either chamber
2. Referred to committee → hearing → markup → vote
3. Full chamber debate and vote
4. Other chamber repeats process
5. Conference committee resolves differences
6. Both chambers vote on final version
7. President signs (becomes law) or vetoes
8. Congress can override veto with 2/3 majority in both chambers

### Regulations (How Agencies Make Rules)
1. Agency proposes rule (published in Federal Register)
2. Public comment period (30–60 days)
3. Agency reviews comments, publishes final rule
4. Rule effective 30 days later (usually)
5. Courts can strike down rules that exceed statutory authority

## Intelligence and Shadow Government

### US Intelligence Community
- 17 agencies including: CIA, NSA, FBI, DIA, NRO, NGA
- **CIA**: Foreign intelligence; covert action
- **NSA**: Signals intelligence (SIGINT); communications surveillance
- **FBI**: Domestic counterintelligence; law enforcement
- **DIA**: Military intelligence
- Director of National Intelligence (DNI): nominal head of IC

### Deep State Concept
- Permanent bureaucratic apparatus that operates independently of elected officials
- Career government employees in defense, intelligence, law enforcement, diplomacy
- Continuity of policy regardless of which party wins elections
- Real phenomenon in the sense of institutional inertia; contested in its malevolent interpretation

## International Governance

### The United Nations
- Founded 1945; 193 member states
- Security Council: US, Russia, China, UK, France (permanent, with veto power) + 10 rotating members
- General Assembly: one country, one vote (no binding authority)
- International Court of Justice: settles disputes between states
- Effectiveness limited by member state sovereignty

### Bretton Woods Institutions
- **World Bank**: Development loans to poor countries
- **IMF**: Financial stability, balance of payments support, structural adjustment
- Created at 1944 Bretton Woods conference to prevent another Great Depression and WWII
- Criticized for imposing austerity conditions that harm populations

### WTO (World Trade Organization)
- Governs international trade rules
- Dispute resolution between member countries
- Replaced GATT (General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade)
- 164 members as of 2024

## Government Finance

### How Governments Fund Themselves
1. **Taxation**: Income, corporate, sales, property, payroll taxes
2. **Debt issuance**: Government bonds (US Treasury bonds, municipal bonds)
3. **Monetary creation**: Central bank can create money (but causes inflation if excessive)
4. **Fees and charges**: Licenses, permits, user fees
5. **State-owned enterprises**: Revenue from government businesses

### Fiscal Policy
- **Expansionary**: Increase spending + cut taxes → stimulate economy
- **Contractionary**: Cut spending + raise taxes → slow inflation
- **Keynesian**: Government should actively use fiscal policy to stabilize the economy
- **Austerity**: Cut spending to reduce deficits (popular with conservatives, controversial in practice)

### US National Debt
- ~$34 trillion (as of 2024)
- ~120% of GDP
- Held by: US public (~70%), foreign governments (~30%)
- Major foreign holders: Japan (~$1.1T), China (~$800B)
- Interest payments: ~$1 trillion/year — becoming the largest federal expenditure
